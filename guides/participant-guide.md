# Participant Guide

## What this is

Three short assessments that work out where your test automation training should start. There is one for test automation fundamentals, one for SQL and one for Python. Each has ten questions and a ten minute timer, and you take each one once.

The point is not to grade you. You are an experienced tester; what this measures is which parts of the programme's prerequisite material you already know, so that your training plan covers what is useful to you and skips what is not. When you finish all three, the application builds your training plan and shows it to you. That plan is the whole output.

It takes under forty minutes in total. You can take the three in any order, and you do not have to do them all in one sitting.

## Signing in

You will be given a participant code and a password, through delivery management.

Your code looks like `participant-07`. It is not your name and not your email address, because the application holds no personal information about you at all. It knows you only as a number. Delivery management keeps the list matching codes to people, and that list never goes into the system.

Enter the code and password on the sign-in page. If you get them wrong ten times within fifteen minutes, sign-in is blocked for that account for a quarter of an hour. Wait, then try once, carefully.

Your session lasts eight hours and extends while you are using it, so you will not be signed out mid-assessment.

## The rules

- **Ten questions, ten minutes, per assessment.** The timer sits at the top right and turns amber when two minutes remain. It starts when you press the button on the instructions screen, and not before.
- **One attempt each.** Once you submit an assessment you cannot take it again.
- **Your answers save themselves.** Every choice is stored on the server as you make it, within about a second. You do not need to save anything.
- **You can move around freely.** Use Previous and Next, and change any answer you like until you submit.
- **Unanswered questions score zero.** There is no penalty for a wrong answer over a blank one, so answer everything.
- **When the timer runs out, the assessment submits itself** with whatever you have saved. Nothing is lost.
- **You can stop and come back.** If you close the browser or lose your connection mid-assessment, sign in again and carry on. Your answers will be there and the clock will show the time that remains, because the timer runs on the server, not in your browser.

## The question types

Two of the types are ordinary. Two use dragging, and both of those can be done entirely without dragging if you prefer or if dragging is awkward on your machine.

**One answer.** Four options, choose one. Click the option.

**More than one answer.** Five or six options, and more than one is right. The screen says "Select all that apply." directly under the question. You get the mark only for selecting exactly the right set, so include every correct option and no others.

**Put things in order.** A short list you arrange into a sequence. The question always says what the ordering criterion is, for example most numerous first, so you never have to guess what is being asked.

- *With the mouse or trackpad:* drag a card by its handle and drop it where you want it.
- *With the buttons:* every card has Move up and Move down buttons. Use those instead of dragging.
- *With the keyboard only:* Tab to a card's handle, press Space to pick it up, use the up and down arrow keys to move it, and press Space again to put it down. Escape cancels a move in progress.

The list starts in a scrambled order, so its starting position is never the answer.

**Match things to categories.** Some items in a tray and three or four labelled categories. Every item belongs in exactly one category.

- *With the mouse or trackpad:* drag an item onto a category. The category highlights as you approach it.
- *With the selector:* every item has a dropdown listing the categories. Choose one. This works with the keyboard and is more precise than dragging.
- *To take an item back:* each placed item has a control to return it to the tray, or set its selector back to Tray.

The question counts as answered only once every item has been placed. The screen shows how many you have placed.

## Reviewing and submitting

From the last question, Review shows all ten as numbered tiles. Answered ones are solid; unanswered ones are outlined in amber. Click any tile to jump back to that question.

Submit asks you to confirm, and reminds you that unanswered questions score zero. After that the assessment is closed.

You then see a short confirmation. It does not show a score, and neither will anything else. That is deliberate: the output of this exercise is your training plan, not a mark.

## Your training plan

Once all three assessments are submitted, your training plan appears. Each module of the programme is listed in the order you would work through it, and each carries one of these:

- **Prescribed.** You take this module. It shows the hours and links straight to the course.
- **Credited.** You have demonstrated this already. Nothing to do.
- **Provisional, confirmed at review.** Provisionally credited, subject to someone confirming real work evidence such as scripts you have written or suites you maintain. Practical automation capability is not credited on a quiz alone, which is why this state exists.
- **Confirmed at journey map issue.** Used for Git essentials, which this exercise does not assess.

At the bottom is the total prescribed learning in hours, and a line saying your journey map will be issued by delivery management.

There is a Print button. It produces a clean single-page A4 version. Keep a copy: it is your record of where you are starting.

Your plan is built from module-level rules, not from a persona. If you are strong in SQL, new to Python and experienced in testing but not in automation, the plan will say exactly that.

## If something goes wrong

Stop, and contact the administrator through delivery management. Say what happened and which assessment you were in.

The administrator can reset an attempt that failed for a technical reason, which lets you start that assessment again with a fresh set of questions. That is the fix for a crashed browser, a dead laptop or a connection that dropped and would not come back.

A few things that look like problems but are not:

| What you see | What is happening |
|---|---|
| You closed the browser by accident | Sign in again. Your answers are saved and the clock shows the time that remains. |
| The timer ran out while you were away | The assessment submitted itself with your saved answers. That is the designed behaviour. |
| You are signed in on two machines | Both work, and the most recent change wins. Use one to avoid confusing yourself. |
| You pressed Submit twice | The second press does nothing. |
| A drag will not start | Use the Move up and Move down buttons, or the category selector. They do exactly the same job. |
| You want to know your score | Scores are not shown to participants at any point. Your training plan is the output. |

If you think a question was wrong or unfair, say so through delivery management. Every question has a written rationale and a source in the course material behind it, and the administrator can look at both. There is no dispute button in the application; it is handled by people.
