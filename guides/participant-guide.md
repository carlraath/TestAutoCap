# Participant Guide

## What this is

Three short assessments that work out where your test automation training should start. There is one for test automation fundamentals, one for SQL and one for Python. Each has ten questions and a ten minute timer, and you take each one once.

The point is not to grade you. You are an experienced tester; what this measures is which parts of the programme's prerequisite material you already know, so that your training plan covers what is useful to you and skips what is not. When you finish all three, the application builds your training plan and shows it to you. That plan is the whole output.

It takes under forty minutes in total. You can take the three in any order, and you do not have to do them all in one sitting.

## Signing in

You will be given a participant code and a password, through delivery management.

Your code looks like `participant-07`. It is not your name and not your email address, because the application holds no personal information about you at all. It knows you only as a number. Delivery management keeps the list matching codes to people, and that list never goes into the system.

The sign-in page asks for two things, Participant code and Password, and has one Sign in button. If either is wrong you get the same message every time, "That participant code and password do not match.", which never says which of the two was at fault.

Ten wrong attempts within fifteen minutes block that code, and the block also applies to the machine you are signing in from. While it is in force even the right password is refused, with that same message. Trying again during the block counts as another failed attempt and starts the fifteen minutes again, so stop, wait a quarter of an hour, then try once, carefully.

Once you are in you land on your assessments: a welcome line addressed to your code, then a card for each of the three showing "10 questions, 10 minutes" and its state, Not started, In progress or Submitted. The button beside it is Start, Continue, or the word Completed once that assessment is done.

Your code and a Sign out link sit at the top right of every screen. Sign out asks you to confirm, and tells you that any assessment in progress keeps its saved answers.

Your session lasts eight hours and extends while you are using it, so you will not be signed out mid-assessment.

## The rules

- **Ten questions, ten minutes, per assessment.** The timer sits at the top right, beside the assessment name, and turns amber once it drops under two minutes. It starts when you press the button on the instructions screen, and not before.
- **One attempt each.** Once you submit an assessment you cannot take it again.
- **Your answers save themselves.** Every choice is stored on the server about a second after you make it. Once you have answered something, a small marker under the question, on the right, reads Saving and then Saved. You do not need to save anything.
- **You can move around freely.** Use Previous and Next, and change any answer you like until you submit.
- **Unanswered questions score zero.** There is no penalty for a wrong answer over a blank one, so answer everything.
- **When the timer runs out, the assessment submits itself** with whatever you have saved. Nothing is lost.
- **You can stop and come back.** If you close the browser or lose your connection mid-assessment, sign in again, press Continue, and carry on. Your answers will be there and the clock will show the time that remains, because the timer runs on the server, not in your browser.

## The question types

Two of the types are ordinary. Two use dragging, and both of those can be done entirely without dragging if you prefer or if dragging is awkward on your machine. The two dragging types appear only in the test automation fundamentals assessment, one of each; SQL and Python are made up of the two ordinary types alone.

**One answer.** Four options, choose one. Click anywhere on the option, or reach the group with Tab and choose with the arrow keys.

**More than one answer.** Five or six options, two or three of which are right. The screen says "Select all that apply." directly under the question. Click anywhere on an option to select it, or reach it with Tab and press Space. You get the mark only for selecting exactly the right set, so include every correct option and no others.

**Put things in order.** Three items you arrange into a sequence. The question always says what the ordering criterion is, for example most numerous at the top of the list, so you never have to guess what is being asked. A line above the list repeats the three ways of doing it.

- *With the mouse or trackpad:* drag a card by the grip handle on its left and drop it where you want it.
- *With the buttons:* every card carries an up arrow and a down arrow on its right. Use those instead of dragging. The top card's up arrow and the bottom card's down arrow are greyed out, because there is nowhere to go.
- *With the keyboard only:* Tab to a card's grip handle, press Space to pick it up, use the up and down arrow keys to move it, and press Space again to put it down. Escape cancels a move in progress and the card goes back where it was.

The number beside each card shows the position it currently holds. The list starts in a scrambled order, so its starting position is never the answer, and the whole sequence has to be right to earn the mark.

**Match things to categories.** Five items in a tray, headed Items, and four labelled categories beside it. Every item belongs in exactly one category, and every category takes at least one item.

- *With the mouse or trackpad:* drag an item onto a category. The category highlights as you approach it.
- *With the selector:* every item has a dropdown listing Tray and the four categories. Choose one. This works with the keyboard and is more precise than dragging.
- *To take an item back:* each placed item has a cross button beside its selector that returns it to the tray, or you can set the selector back to Tray.

The question counts as answered only once every item has been placed, and every item has to be in the right category to earn the mark. The count at the top of the tray, for example "3 of 5 placed", tells you how far along you are.

## Reviewing and submitting

From the last question, Review shows all ten as numbered tiles. Answered ones are solid; unanswered ones are outlined in amber, and a small key underneath says which is which. Select any tile to jump back to that question. Underneath, a line counts them, for example "8 answered, 2 unanswered", followed by the reminder that unanswered questions score zero. Back to questions returns you to where you were.

Submit assessment opens a confirmation box headed "Submit this assessment" that repeats "Unanswered questions score zero. Submit now?" and offers Cancel and Submit. After Submit the assessment is closed.

You then see a short confirmation: "Your [assessment] assessment has been submitted.", and a line telling you your training plan appears once all three are complete. It does not show a score, and neither will anything else. That is deliberate: the output of this exercise is your training plan, not a mark.

## Your training plan

Once all three assessments are submitted, the confirmation offers View your training plan, and your assessments page becomes the plan as well. Each module of the programme is listed in the order you would work through it, and each is marked in one of four ways:

- **Prescribed.** You take this module. It is the solid coloured row: it carries the module name, a link straight to the course, and the hours. There is no other wording on it, because the row itself is the instruction.
- **Credited.** Marked "Credited" with a tick. You have demonstrated this already, so there is nothing to do and no course is listed.
- **Provisional, confirmed at review.** Marked in exactly those words. Provisionally credited, subject to someone confirming real work evidence such as scripts you have written or suites you maintain. Practical automation capability is not credited on a quiz alone, which is why this state exists.
- **Confirmed at journey map issue.** Used for GIT-1 Git essentials, which this exercise does not assess.

At the bottom is your total, written as "Prescribed learning: N hours." with N adding up the Prescribed rows only, and then the line "Your journey map will be issued by delivery management."

There is a Print button below the plan. It produces a clean single-page A4 version with the header, footer and the button itself dropped out. Keep a copy: it is your record of where you are starting.

Your plan is built from module-level rules, not from a persona. If you are strong in SQL, new to Python and experienced in testing but not in automation, the plan will say exactly that.

## If something goes wrong

Stop, and contact the administrator through delivery management. Say what happened and which assessment you were in.

The administrator can reset an attempt that failed for a technical reason. That assessment returns to Not started, and starting it again gives you a fresh set of questions and the full ten minutes. That is the fix for a crashed browser, a dead laptop or a connection that dropped and would not come back.

A few things that look like problems but are not:

| What you see | What is happening |
|---|---|
| You closed the browser by accident | Sign in again and press Continue. Your answers are saved and the clock shows the time that remains. |
| The timer ran out while you were away | The assessment submitted itself with your saved answers, timed at the moment the clock hit zero. That is the designed behaviour. |
| You are signed in on two machines | Both work, and the most recent change wins. Use one to avoid confusing yourself. |
| You pressed Submit twice | The second press changes nothing and returns the same confirmation. |
| A drag will not start | Use the up and down arrows on each card, or the category selector. They do exactly the same job. |
| You want to know your score | Scores are not shown to participants at any point. Your training plan is the output. |

If you think a question was wrong or unfair, say so through delivery management. Every question has a written rationale and a source in the course material behind it, and the administrator can look at both. There is no dispute button in the application; it is handled by people.
